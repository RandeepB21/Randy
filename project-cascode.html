Telescopic cascode amplifier

A cascode gain stage pushed to about 90 dB, then buffered so it can actually drive a load without giving all that bandwidth away.

Design notes

Biasing

Stacking four devices leaves little headroom, so the bias points were worked out by hand first and then tuned against the LTspice operating point until every transistor sat in saturation with margin.

Driving a load

The cascode's high output resistance is what produces the gain, but it also forms a very low-frequency pole with any real load. Adding a source-follower buffer separates the high-impedance node from the 2 pF load and extended the bandwidth by about 38×.

Gain versus bandwidth

I compared the full cascode load with a simplified PMOS load. The simpler load gives up gain in exchange for a lower output resistance and a higher dominant pole, which makes the gain–bandwidth trade-off visible in the same testbench.
