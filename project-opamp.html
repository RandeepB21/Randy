Two-stage Miller-compensated op-amp

A PMOS-input two-stage OTA, sized for a uniform 0.2 V overdrive and compensated so the right-half-plane zero disappears instead of eating phase margin.

Design notes

Sizing

I fixed every device at VOV = 0.2 V and set IREF = 20 µA, which gives roughly 10 µA per input device and about 41 µA in the second stage. Widths then follow from the square-law relation and the PDK's process parameters, and the LTspice operating point puts every saturated device within about 10 mV of the 0.2 V target.

Compensation

Miller compensation splits the poles but introduces a right-half-plane zero at Gm2/Cc, which adds phase lag exactly where it hurts. Placing a nulling resistor Rz = 1/Gm2 in series with Cc pushes that zero to infinity. With Cc ≈ 460 fF the result is a 60.6° phase margin at a 29 MHz GBW.

Scaling check

Doubling the bias current roughly doubled ft and halved the required Rz, as expected from gm ∝ ID/VOV at a fixed overdrive. Seeing the simulator follow the first-order equations this closely was a useful sanity check on the whole design.
